LLM-ACES: Closed-Loop Discovery of Dynamical Systems with LLM-Guided Adaptive Search

Nikhil Abhyankar, Sha Li, Sanchit Kabra, Naren Ramakrishnan, Yulia Gel, Chandan K. Reddy
Virginia Tech
NeurIPS 2026
Paper arXiv Code

Abstract

Recovering governing Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) from data is a central challenge in modeling dynamical systems across scientific domains. Existing approaches cast discovery as a static inference problem over fixed datasets, assuming that the observed trajectories are sufficiently informative. However, dynamical systems evolve over large state spaces, and limited data can make multiple equations observationally indistinguishable, leading to identifiability gaps and the recovery of incorrect governing equations. To address this, we introduce LLM-ACES, or LLM-guided Active Closed-loop Equation Search, a closed-loop framework that jointly optimizes symbolic hypothesis construction and adaptive data acquisition. In LLM-ACES, a large language model (LLM) proposes operator priors that partition the large search space into distinct regions, within which candidate equations are fit to the observed data. The disagreement among these candidates guides the acquisition of informative trajectories, creating a feedback loop that iteratively refines both the hypothesis space and the discovered dynamics. On 122 ODE systems spanning ODEBench and ODEBase, LLM-ACES achieves the lowest median NMSE, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by several orders of magnitude while achieving a high symbolic accuracy of 46.2% and 52.4%, respectively. Our analysis further shows that LLM-ACES is sample-efficient, achieving better performance with one-tenth the data. Furthermore, LLM-ACES’s feedback-driven data acquisition makes it robust to noise and recovers the correct symbolic structure, while baselines introduce spurious terms that fit the data locally but obscure the true governing relationships.

Why Passive Data Is Not Enough

When training trajectories cover only part of the state space, structurally different equations fit the data equally well. The paper illustrates this with dx/dt = x + 0.1x³: sampled at small x, the cubic term is invisible, so passive methods return a linear law with negligible training error. Method Overview

LLM-ACES treats discovery as a closed loop in which the symbolic hypothesis space and the acquired dataset co-evolve. The LLM never writes the final equation; it shapes the space that symbolic regression searches, and the fitted candidates decide which experiment to run next.

A

LLM-guided hypothesis construction

Each round the LLM proposes K = 3 operator priors (sets of unary and binary operators): one exploits the best patterns in the experience buffer, the others explore operator families not yet tried.

B

Hypothesis generation

PySR fits a candidate equation for every state dimension inside each constrained subspace. The accumulated population is a structured picture of what the dynamics could be.

C

Active experiment selection

Every candidate is rolled out from a pool of initial conditions; the oracle is queried where the average pairwise NMSE between rollouts, the predictive divergence, is largest.

D

Evaluation and feedback

New trajectories are split into train and validation data and all candidates are re-scored. The two best and two worst per dimension become demonstrations and failure cases in the next prompt.

Qualitative Results

Final equations for the Schnackenberg and Maxwell–Bloch systems. Green marks components that match the ground truth; red marks spurious terms.

Passive regression oversimplifies

PySR and SINDy return sparse or nearly linear expressions that drop the essential nonlinear interaction terms, even with low error on the observed trajectory.

Richer searches invent terms

Operon and ODEFormer add spurious polynomial or oscillatory terms (ODEFormer even inserts a sine) that improve local interpolation while obscuring the mechanism.

Acquisition alone is not identifiability

LLM-ODE recovers some components but misses coupled interactions, and APPS-ODE acquires data yet still returns oversimplified equations: the queried trajectories must separate competing hypotheses.

LLM-ACES recovers the mechanism

Querying where candidate equations disagree, inside LLM-shaped operator spaces, yields compact and correct equations, including the reaction term of Schnackenberg and the bilinear couplings of Maxwell–Bloch.
